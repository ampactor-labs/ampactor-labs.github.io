# The upgrade audit

An audit of ampactor.dev for improvements and upgrades, November 18, 2026,
at `origin/main` 369e3bf. Four passes ran side by side:

1. **Content:** every cartridge, the résumé, the noscript list and the page
   heads, checked against each project's README, its code and every link.
2. **Accessibility and UX:** a real browser, every screen, nine viewport
   sizes, zoom, forced colours and reduced motion.
3. **Code, tests, dependencies and CI:** including the Actions history.
4. **Performance, search and the platform:** plus the upgrades in section 7.

The findings that lead each section were reproduced or re-checked a second
time before they went in.

Two rules from `CLAUDE.md` shaped every recommendation:

- The cabinet stays the whole page. Nothing here puts a page in front of it,
  around it or in place of it, or brings back the title card.
- The words are Morgan's. A claim that is false or stale is flagged with its
  evidence, and no replacement wording is written for it.

Effort: **S** is under half a day, **M** one to three days, **L** a week or
more.

## The short version

- **The site cannot deploy.**
  - Every deploy since November 16 has failed, and the live site is the
    November 15 build.
  - The cause: GitHub's `ubuntu-latest` runner is now Ubuntu 26.04, and the
    locked Playwright 1.56.1 cannot install Chromium on it.
  - The fix is one line per workflow.
- **A tab left open across a deploy breaks.**
  - Nothing catches a script file that fails to load, and nightly deploys
    rename files almost every night.
  - The cabinet is replaced by the boot shim's "⚠ boot error" dump.
- **Keyboard users can't follow the links on the first screen.**
  - Enter or Space on a focused RESUME, GITHUB, LINKEDIN, email or phone link
    opens the lit cartridge instead.
  - On a readout, Enter on a focused SOURCE opens the lit DEMO.
- **At 200% zoom on a laptop, the project list is 0 px tall.**
  - The header and the deck fill the screen.
  - A landscape phone shows no cartridges either.
- **axe passes, but that hides contrast failures.**
  - On gradients, axe files the contrast checks as "incomplete", and the
    browser tests fail only on violations.
  - Measured from rendered pixels, the tag chips fail on 12 of 21
    cartridges, INSERT COIN fails, and so does the résumé's grey text.
- **MENTL is the most out-of-date cartridge.**
  - Its DEMO link is a GitHub Pages 404.
  - Its program listing teaches the old meaning of `><`.
  - Its operator note lists one known false proof where the README lists
    three.
- **About fifteen other claims on the cards don't match their READMEs.**
  - TURBOSORT shows speedups its README says did not reproduce, because the
    README sync reads a stale `master` branch.
  - The project count is wrong in four places: it says 19 or 18, and there
    are 21.
- **The site is hard to find.**
  - A web search for "Morgan Espitia software engineer" or "ampactor.dev"
    returns nothing about Morgan or the site.
  - The sitemap has 5 URLs. Every page shares one social card.
  - The 21 cartridges have no URLs of their own, because they are fragments
    of `/arcade/`.
- **Performance is good.**
  - On a simulated phone, Lighthouse scores the home page 95, the commit log
    97 and the case study 98.
  - The home page's largest paint is 2.52 s, which is the boot printing
    Morgan's name.
- **The biggest opportunity is showing the work instead of describing it.**
  - 15 of the 21 cartridges are live web apps on this same origin, and none
    blocks embedding. The cabinet could play them in the tube.
  - Mentl's compiler is one WebAssembly file, so the MENTL cartridge could
    run its own listing.
  - Section 7 has these and eight more.

## 3. Accessibility and UX

axe reports no violations on most screens, and that result is misleading.
The cabinet, /craft/ and /receipts/ sit on gradients, so axe files almost
every contrast check as "incomplete": 28 to 63 elements per cabinet screen,
187 on /craft/ and 265 on /receipts/. The browser tests fail only on
violations, so contrast there is never tested. The violations axe did find:

- the résumé: contrast, a link told apart by colour alone, and no landmarks;
- the hidden synth: 8 unlabelled sliders;
- the commit log's chart tables: 3 scroll areas a keyboard can't reach.

The rest below was measured in the browser: keyboard walks, simulated zoom
from 150% to 400%, forced colours, reduced motion, no JavaScript, and text
contrast sampled from rendered pixels. Nine viewport sizes were used, from
320×640 to 1920×1080, plus a landscape phone. The two worst problems,
keyboard links and zoom, are items 3 and 4 in section 1.

### Usability

- **Small type on phones.** At 375 to 412 px wide, readout text is 11 to
  12 px, specifications 10 to 11 px and tags 8 to 9 px. That is below the
  design doc's own 12 px minimum for sans text. Desktop text is 14 px.
- **The first visit.**
  - The list appears about 4.0 s after the page starts loading (2.7 s with
    reduced motion).
  - "[ TAP TO SKIP ]" appears twice, and says TAP on a desktop.
  - During the BIOS, the deck keys can be tabbed to and are announced as
    working buttons, but they do nothing.
- **Two rings that look alike on a readout.** The lit link's ring (the
  project colour) and the focus ring are both cyan on SONIDO.
- **Home, End, Page Up and Page Down** scroll the list without moving the
  selection. The lit row leaves the view, and the next ▼ jumps back to the
  top.
- **A does nothing on the hidden programs,** though its label says OPEN.
- **The TUNNEL_RUN deep link** creates an AudioContext before any gesture and
  logs eight warnings. Every other screen's console was clean.
- **The cabinet sits off centre on Windows and Linux.**
  `scrollbar-gutter: stable` (`global.css:13`) reserves 15 px for a scrollbar
  on a page that never scrolls. With classic scrollbars that is a dark stripe
  on the right, and the cabinet is 7.5 px left of centre.
- **Rows visible:**

  | Viewport | Rows visible |
  | --- | --- |
  | 320×640 | 1.5 |
  | 375×548 | 2 |
  | Pixel 7 | 7 |
  | 1366×768 | 5 |
  | iPad | 8 |
  | 1920×1080 | 9 |

- **Responsiveness is good.** Measured from key press to paint, moving
  through the list takes at most 72 ms. Opening a cartridge takes 104 to
  136 ms, including on a Pixel 7 with the CPU slowed four times.

Copy that a screen reader or a sighted visitor trips on (Morgan's words,
flagged only):

- HIGH SCORES says "LAST TWELVE MONTHS" over nine bars.
- The CREDITS row is read as "CREDITS —", because its subtitle is empty.
- COHERENCE FIELD's language chip is a lone "—".
- Glyphs are read aloud: ▸, ›, ●, >>, "[ Ⓑ BACK ]" and 🔒.
- The near-invisible tagline stencil is the first thing read on every
  readout.
- The deck's A-mark is an unnamed image on every screen.

Keep:

- **The list:** the listbox pattern (`aria-activedescendant`, named options,
  `aria-selected`), and Back returning focus to the list with the row lit and
  the scroll kept.
- **Links and the deck:** deep links skip the boot, every deck control is a
  named button, SOUND is a real switch, and touch targets are 44 px.
- **Quiet and still where asked:** no live-region spam, no AudioContext
  before a gesture (except the deep link above), SOUND OFF that silences
  everything and is remembered, reduced motion honoured everywhere, and no
  flashing.
- **The commit log:** real tables with `aria-sort`, a polite "Showing N of M"
  live region, and a native `<dialog>` that returns focus.
- **The fallbacks and pages:** a plain, complete noscript list, a résumé with
  real headings and the browser's focus ring, and no viewport setting that
  blocks zoom.

- **The home page's largest paint is the boot.** It is the BIOS line
  "OPERATOR: MORGAN ESPITIA", with 2.06 s of render delay. That is the design,
  and /craft/ says so. It sits just over the repo's own 2.5 s gate, so
  `npm run audit` would fail on a host this slow.
- **The "preload-helper" chunk** (100 KB, 40 KB gzip) is GSAP core,
  `projects.js`, `useCabinetState` and the intro hooks.
  - It exists because `DetailScreen.jsx:2` imports `detailLinksOf` from
    `useCabinetState.js`. That makes the hook's whole module graph, GSAP
    included, a set shared between the entry and a lazy chunk.
  - Moving `detailLinksOf` next to the data dissolves it.
  - The cabinet pages load ten module scripts, where the comment in
    `vite.config.js` describes three.
- **Dependencies that cost more than they do:**
  - zod is 22.8 KB gzip, about 40% of the commit log's chunk, to validate a
    five-field export form. A hand check, `zod/mini` or a lazily loaded form
    would remove most of it.
  - GSAP is 19.6 KB gzip for four tweens, downloaded by returning visitors
    who never see the intro. Load it on a first visit only, or use the Web
    Animations API.
  - `@gsap/react` is unused.
- **Caching.** GitHub Pages sends `cache-control: max-age=600` for every
  file, hashed assets and fonts included, so after ten minutes a returning
  visitor revalidates everything. Upgrades 7 and 11 fix this.
- **The tunnel on desktop.**
  - It draws at the display's refresh rate: 120 times a second on a
    ProMotion MacBook.
  - In this container (no GPU, software compositing), the idle cabinet went
    from 0% main-thread busy under reduced motion to 41% at 1280×800 and 52%
    at 1920×1080. 2560×1440 at 2× dropped to 34 frames a second.
  - Script was about 1% of that; the rest was compositing.
  - A machine with GPU canvas acceleration pays much less. This is the case
    for machines rendering in software (virtual machines, remote desktops,
    blocklisted drivers).
  - A 30 fps cap halves it at 60 Hz and saves three quarters at 120 Hz, with
    no visible difference at this speed. Upgrade 5 removes it.
- **`og-home.png` is 384 KB.** Fine for crawlers, but it can be a quarter of
  that.

## 6. Code health, tests and CI

The architecture is in good shape. Keep these:

- the URL as the source of truth: the pure `arcadeRoute.ts`, one owner of
  history, and intents;
- the pure, 97%-covered ledger model;
- storage wrapped in `try` everywhere but one place;
- an AudioContext that waits for a gesture and sleeps when hidden;
- injection-safe leaderboard input;
- one table that renders every page head;
- no third-party requests, guarded by a browser test;
- e2e tests written as visitors.

What to change, by impact:

**CI and deploy**

- **e2e never gates production.**
  - CI ignores main, and `deploy.yml` re-runs lint, typecheck and the unit
    tests but never the browser tests.
  - main takes direct pushes; 369e3bf deployed without an e2e run.
  - Fix: one pipeline where `deploy` has `needs: [check, e2e]` and is the
    only job holding `pages: write` and `id-token: write`. Today one job
    holds them through `npm ci` and the network-fetching build. Then require
    CI on main. M.
- **CI runs twice per PR push.** It triggers on `push` to non-main branches
  and on `pull_request`, and the concurrency group is keyed on `github.ref`,
  which differs between the two. Trigger `push` on main only, or group on
  `github.head_ref || github.ref`. S.
- **Action versions.**
  - The logs warn that `checkout`, `setup-node`, `cache` and
    `upload-artifact` v4 target the deprecated Node 20.
  - Move to current majors, pinned by SHA in the jobs that can write.
  - Add Dependabot or Renovate, cache the Playwright browser, and set
    `timeout-minutes`. S.
- **Checks that exist but never run in CI:** `resume:check`,
  `sync:readmes:check` (which reports stale content today) and
  `format:check`. Lint warnings don't fail the build; one of the four is a
  real bug (below). Add `--max-warnings=0`. S.
- **The nightly cron** is set for 09:17 UTC but starts 6 to 10 hours late,
  and it redeploys when nothing changed.
- **The leaderboard is forgeable.**
  - Anyone with a GitHub account can file `[tunnel-run] ZZZ 9999999`
    without playing, and each accepted score is a commit to main and a
    production deploy.
  - Ties lose and scores cap at seven digits, so ten forged maximums lock
    the board for good.
  - A burst of three submissions drops the middle one.
  - Fix: auto-accept only the owner's and collaborators' issues, leaving the
    rest for a maintainer's label. Keep one row per user, publish with the
    nightly deploy, and give the reply step `if: always()`. S-M.
  - Today the board holds two scores since July, both Morgan's.

**Code**

- **TypeScript where it pays.**
  - The arcade is about 7,600 lines of JavaScript plus 1,270 of data, and
    `checkJs` is off. `src/data/types.ts` is imported by nothing.

## 7. The upgrades

The cabinet already says who Morgan is and lists the work. What it can't do
yet is show the work. Most of these upgrades move the projects themselves
onto the tube. Each fits inside the cabinet as a cartridge, a screen or a
control; none adds a page in front of it.

### 1. Every cartridge becomes a real page, with its own picture (L)

**What.**

- `/arcade/wasatch/` instead of `/arcade/#wasatch`, built as a real file.
- Each one carries its own title, description, canonical URL and social
  card.
- The readout's text is in the HTML before any JavaScript runs.
- The cabinet still opens and closes cartridges the same way. The router
  uses paths instead of fragments, and old `#` links redirect.

**Why.**

- Shared and searched links are how a portfolio travels, and right now
  every link to every project previews as the same picture of the list.
- This turns one indexable page of work into 22.
- It also does the next step /craft/ promises ("prerender each page's text
  at build time").

**How.**

- `site.js` already renders every head. Add one entry per project, and
  prerender `DetailScreen` with React's `react-dom/static` in a Vite SSR
  pass.
- GitHub Pages serves real files, so deep links work without an SPA
  fallback.
- `scripts/render-og.mjs` already photographs the cabinet with Playwright;
  run it once per open cartridge.
- Add every cartridge and the 15 hosted apps to the sitemap, and the JSON-LD
  from section 5.

### 2. Each readout shows the thing itself (M)

**What.**

- A still of the live project, captured at build time, in the readout.
- On desktop it fills the empty right third of the readout (the text column
  stops at about 520 px). On a phone it sits under the header.
- Where motion tells the story, it can be a muted four-second loop.

**Why.**

- Today a recruiter reads about Wasatch Symphony's trains and seismometers
  and sees none of them.
- One picture is the fastest evidence on the page, and it is fact, not
  copy, so it fits the one-fact-per-screen rule.

**How.**

- A build script visits each `live` URL with Playwright and writes AVIF
  stills (and AV1/WebM loops) to `public/cartridges/`.
- The same capture feeds upgrade 1's social cards.
- Projects with no web demo (turbosort, clob, landed, the APK) get a
  terminal capture or a frame of their own output.

### 3. Insert the cartridge: the demo plays in the tube (M-L)

**What.**

- A on DEMO loads the live app inside the CRT, in place of the readout.
- The scanlines and glass stay on top, and B ejects it.
- Opening it in a new tab stays available.

**Why.**

- This is what the cabinet is for: the cartridges are games and tools, and
  they are on the same origin.
- All 15 hosted apps answer 200 with no `X-Frame-Options` or
  `frame-ancestors`.

**How.**

- A same-origin `<iframe>` in the tube. Because it is same-origin, the
  cabinet can listen to the frame's own keys, so B and Escape still eject
  while the app has focus.
- In Chromium, Speculation Rules can prerender the lit cartridge's demo, so
  A is instant.
- **Care points:**
  - Apps that need a tap to start audio handle that themselves.
  - The apps share the cabinet's storage.
  - Fix the apapacho cache bug (section 1, item 11) first.
  - On a phone the demo should take the whole tube.

### 4. MENTL runs its own listing (spike S, ship M-L)

**What.** A RUN button under the hello listing compiles `five.mn` with the
real compiler and prints its output into the tube. An edit mode makes it a
small playground.

**Why.**

- It turns the most abstract cartridge into the most convincing one: the
  operators can be watched working.
- It also fixes the drifting listing for good, because the listing becomes
  the file.

**How.**

- The compiler is one WebAssembly module, `boot/mentl.wasm`: 4.9 MB, or 1.2
  MB gzip, loaded only on RUN. It imports WASI preview 1 and nothing else.
- Mentl Space already runs it in a worker (`ide/wheel-worker.js`), so most
  of the host exists.
- **Limits:**
  - It needs WebAssembly tail calls: Chrome 112+, Firefox 121+, Safari 18.2+.
  - Mentl Space notes it needs cross-origin isolation for shared memory;
    this one program uses none.
- **Cheaper first step:** a Mentl Space link that opens `five.mn`. That needs
  a URL parameter in Mentl Space itself.

### 5. A GPU tube (L)

**What.** One WebGL2 pass draws the tunnel and the CRT together: scanlines,
bloom, barrel curvature, phosphor persistence and vignette. It runs in a
worker through `OffscreenCanvas`, capped at 30 fps when idle.

**Why.**

- The always-on desktop tunnel becomes nearly free.
- The tube reads as curved glass rather than layered CSS.
- The main thread is left for input.

**How.**

- Keep the current 2D frame as the fallback, and draw one still frame under
  reduced motion.
- The CSS CRT layers in `crtStyles.js` become shader uniforms.

### 6. Real controllers (S-M)

**What.**

- A USB or Bluetooth gamepad drives the cabinet, through the Gamepad API:
  - the d-pad walks the list;
  - A and B open and step back;
  - Start inserts the coin.
- TUNNEL_RUN plays with a stick.
- The on-screen buttons press in step with the real ones.
- A short vibration on A and B on Android phones.

**Why.** It is an arcade cabinet, and the site has no gamepad or haptics
code today.

**How.** One polling loop maps the gamepad to the same intents the keyboard
sends.

### 7. An installable cabinet (M)

**What.**

- A web app manifest (`display: fullscreen`, portrait) and a service
  worker.
- On a phone the cabinet installs to the home screen and opens without
  browser chrome, giving the tube the whole screen.
- Repeat visits load from cache and work offline.

**Why.**

- It beats Pages' ten-minute cache.
- It keeps the old chunks an open tab still needs, which also covers
  section 1, item 2.

**How.**

- Cache-first for hashed `/assets/` and `/fonts/`; network-first for HTML.
- The one hard rule: 15 other apps live on this origin, so the worker must
  handle only the portfolio's own paths and pass everything else through
  untouched.

### 8. A leaderboard people can use (M)

**What.**

- Submitting a score without a GitHub account.
- Optionally, a score that has to be replayed to count.

**Why.** Two entries in four months, both Morgan's, suggests the
GitHub-issue path is too much friction for a visitor.

**How.**

- The `ampactor.dev` zone is already on Cloudflare (the `mentl` and `space`
  subdomains answer from it), so a Worker with D1 or KV, rate-limited by IP,
  is a small step.
- Once the game step is pure (section 6), a submission can carry its seed
  and inputs, and the Worker can re-run them.
- Harden the issue path either way (section 6).

### 9. Know who plays (S)

**What.**

- Cookieless analytics: GoatCounter, or Cloudflare Web Analytics.
- A few events:
  - a cartridge opened;
  - DEMO, SOURCE and RESUME pressed;
  - the coin inserted.
- Real-user Web Vitals, and a beacon when a chunk fails to load.

**Why.**

- Today there is no way to know whether anyone opens a cartridge, or where
  visitors stop.
- Without cookies there is no consent banner, so nothing goes in front of the
  cabinet.

### 10. Truth on autopilot (M)

**What.** A nightly job that fails loudly when the site stops being true:

- every link answers;
- every number in a highlight appears in its README;
- MENTL's listing equals its file;
- the counts come from the data;
- `sync:readmes:check` and `resume:check` pass;
- Lighthouse runs and writes the result /craft/ shows, with its date.

**Why.**

- `CLAUDE.md` says every claim is literally true and checkable. Today that
  is checked by hand, and section 2 is what drifted in five weeks.
- The dead MENTL link would have been caught the night it broke.

### 11. An edge in front of Pages (S, settings only)

**What.** Turn on Cloudflare's proxy for the apex, which today points straight
at GitHub Pages while the subdomains already go through Cloudflare. Then:

- real response headers: a Content-Security-Policy, HSTS, and
  `immutable` caching for `/assets/` and `/fonts/`;
- Early Hints;
- analytics with no script.

**Tradeoff.** TLS runs in "Full" mode in front of GitHub's certificate, and
the HTML cache must stay short.

`vite preview`, three runs per page, with the mobile profile.
